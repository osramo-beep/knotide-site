Starting point

A well-defined problem in animal health, still unresolved after decades

Animal production faces parasites and pathogens for which the available solutions are increasingly ineffective, costly, or prone to resistance over time. Sea lice (Lepeophtheirus salmonis, Caligus rogercresseyi) affecting salmon aquaculture are a clear example of this type of challenge.

This is Knotide Bio's first application case: demonstrating, on a real and measurable problem, how a well-engineered biomolecule can replace approaches worn down by resistance.

An industry under constant pressure Sea lice control has been estimated at around one billion US dollars per year in direct treatment costs, with indirect losses — mortality and reduced production — estimated at several times that figure, according to industry estimates cited in EU-funded research projects.

Platform

From biomolecule to product, with scientific rigour at every stage

Knotide Bio explores families of bioactive peptides and proteins — including ICK peptides (knottins), VNARs and nanobodies (VHH) — as the starting point for designing biotechnological solutions directed at specific targets in animal health.

  1. 01

    Discovery

    Bioprospecting and selection of biomolecules with potential activity against identified targets.

  2. 02

    Characterisation

    Study of the structure and function of selected molecules, and their mechanism of action.

  3. 03

    Validation

    Laboratory assays and analyses confirming activity, selectivity and safety.

  4. 04

    Application

    Formulation and integration of validated biomolecules into solutions for animal health, nutrition and welfare.

AI-guided molecular design

Knotide Bio develops small, highly stable biological molecules — bioactive peptides designed with the support of AI-based tools to selectively block critical infection and adhesion steps, without relying on direct toxicity.

This design approach favours precision over broad-spectrum action: molecules are shaped to fit a specific interaction surface, reducing off-target effects and the selective pressure that drives resistance.

Applications

Three complementary pillars in animal production

Animal health

Biomolecules directed at parasites and pathogens relevant to animal production, offering an alternative to resistance-prone solutions.

Animal nutrition

Bioactive peptides and proteins applied in nutritional solutions that support animal performance and gut health.

Animal welfare

Solutions that reduce the need for intensive handling and treatment, with direct benefit for animal welfare.

Initial focus

Knotide Bio's first application case is sea lice in salmon aquaculture — a problem with significant economic impact and no dominant biotechnological solution — a natural entry point to validate the platform before extending it to other species and other parasites.

Team

A founding team built on deep research and industry experience

Knotide Bio was founded by three senior researchers with complementary academic and industry backgrounds in biochemical engineering and biotechnology.

3 PhDs in Biochemical Engineering & Biotechnology
40+ years of combined industry & R&D experience
€25M+ in managed R&D funding

The founding team holds doctorates in biochemical engineering and biotechnology from Portuguese and international institutions, together with postgraduate training in business management. Combined, the team brings more than four decades of experience in industrial innovation, R&D project leadership, and national and European funding, including FCT, Horizon Europe and COMPETE2020/2030 programmes.

Their backgrounds span antibody and nanobody (VHH) engineering at organisations including Ablynx — the company that pioneered VHH technology — and Fairjourney Biologics, as well as collaborative research with academic and industry partners such as Queen Mary University of London, Johnson & Johnson and Abbott. Across their careers, the team has led physico-chemical, structural and formulation analysis platforms, managed R&D projects from scientific conception through to industrial execution, and directed a multi-year biotechnology programme valued at over €25 million.

Current and recent roles span R&D leadership in the biotechnology industry, peptide and nanobody discovery, and coordination of a university-based biomolecules innovation group, providing a direct institutional channel for scientific partnerships. The team's technical competence covers both core biomolecule families explored by Knotide Bio: nanobodies and knottins.
